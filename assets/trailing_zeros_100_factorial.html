How many zeros are at the end of 100!?

An interactive visualization of trailing zeros in factorials.
The key idea
A trailing zero comes from a factor of 10 = 2 × 5. In a factorial, factors of 2 are abundant, so the limiting factor is how many 5s appear in the prime factorization.

Where do the factors of 5 come from?
Multiples of 5 contribute at least one factor of 5. Multiples of 25 contain an extra factor of 5.

For 100!
⌊100 / 5⌋ = 20 + ⌊100 / 25⌋ = 4 = 24
The four numbers 25, 50, 75, and 100 each contribute a second factor of 5. So merely counting the 20 multiples of 5 would miss four additional zeros.
